# From a hand-drawn sketch to a live WhatsApp flow

How I turned a jewellery retailer's sketch into a working customer flow, and delivered their storefront and support desk.

| 1 | 2 | 1 |
|---|---|---|
| hand-drawn sketch as the brief | systems: storefront and WhatsApp flow | rollback path kept ready |

- **Client:** a jewellery retailer with physical stores
- **My role:** Technical Project Manager and developer
- **Timeline:** 2026

## The brief was a drawing

The owner wanted a guided WhatsApp conversation for customers. The requirement arrived as a hand-drawn sketch of that conversation.

## How I turned it into a requirement

1. I converted the sketch into a WhatsApp flow on the EnOrbit platform.
2. Where the sketch was unclear, I wrote down the assumption I made, so the client could correct it instead of discovering it later.
3. I kept a rollback path, so the previous flow could be restored if the new one didn't work for customers.
4. Then I went live.

## The storefront and support desk

Alongside the flow, I delivered the retailer's website and operations tools:
- a product catalogue and a store locator
- customer enquiries
- support tickets with response-time targets and escalation
- a knowledge base and content management
- a role matrix, so each staff member sees only what they need
- analytics

## What I'd tell another TPM

A sketch is a real requirement. Don't wait for a formal spec: build from the sketch, write down every assumption you made, and keep a way back.
