# A support CRM that 15 people used every day, and why it was switched off

What I learned from software that worked and was still retired.

| ~15 | 6 | 1 |
|---|---|---|
| staff using it daily | modules: agents, partners, bulk orders, complaints, logs, analytics | business pivot that retired it |

- **Company:** The Business Wings
- **My role:** Senior Project Manager; I delivered the CRM
- **Timeline:** 2025

## What it did

I delivered an internal support CRM with:
- agent accounts
- partner and restaurant records
- bulk orders
- complaints and activity logs
- analytics for the managers

## What happened

About 15 staff used it every day for several months. Then the business moved to a different model, and the workflows the CRM supported stopped existing. It was switched off.

## What I took from it

The software worked, and people used it. It still lost its purpose, because it was tied to one way of making money.

The lesson: before anything is designed, ask
- how does the business make money from this?
- what happens to this software if that changes?

## What I'd tell another TPM

Adoption is not the finish line. Write down which business process the software depends on, so everyone can see the risk if that process changes.
